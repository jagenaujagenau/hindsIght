# Copy direction

## The job

**When something worth remembering comes up unexpectedly, help me keep it after I realize it matters, so I can stay involved instead of interrupting the moment to capture it.**

This is not a productivity dashboard or a promise of perfect memory. It is a little more room to be present.

- Functional progress: recover a name, instruction, recommendation, or thought without having started a recording beforehand.
- Emotional progress: move from “I’ve lost it” to “I can still keep it.” Less anticipation, less regret.
- Social progress: stay in the conversation instead of reaching for a phone or asking someone to repeat themselves. Respect the other person’s consent.

## Story

1. Recognition: you only know afterward.
2. Permission: **Just be here.**
3. Reassurance: unsaved audio disappears; this is not a whole-life archive.
4. Realization: **Wait. That mattered.**
5. Relief: **Keep it.** You can come back later.
6. Retrieval: the name escapes you; the moment doesn’t.
7. Agency: choose how far back to remember.
8. Trust: personal audio, local processing, inspectable open-source code.
9. Release: **You don’t have to know it matters. Not yet.**

## Language rules

- Lead with a recognizable situation, not an architecture term.
- Use ordinary words: “within reach,” “how far back,” “your phone has it.”
- Be warm without manufacturing sentiment. A restaurant recommendation, a family recipe, and a thought on a walk carry more meaning than abstract claims about memories.
- Explain the listening session and limited window. Never imply recovery before listening starts or beyond the selected window.
- Preserve real app UI strings; simplify the surrounding website copy instead.
- Keep limitations findable. Put encoding/storage specifications and transcript prerequisites in labeled, keyboard-accessible disclosures.
- Keep fictional sample data and silent playback clearly labeled.
- Use SVG icons, not emoji or typographic stand-ins for controls. Punctuation, timestamps, and multiplication signs in playback speeds are text, not icons.
- MIT licensing belongs in the repository documentation. The website invites people to explore, adapt, and contribute without legal housekeeping copy.
